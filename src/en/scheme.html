PINGLISH / a reversible romanization for Standard Chinese

One sound, one spelling.

No ü. No tone marks. No y/w glides. No iu/ui/un contractions. No j/q/x. Every character is on the keyboard, and every spelling can be parsed back into pinyin.

See the tables ↓ Try the converter Download syllables.json

This is a design exercise, not an official standard. Its value is not “this is how it should be”, but the proof that another set of constraints produces a different, coherent answer.

00 / 07

Six principles.

Set the constraints first, then argue about spelling. Each one answers a compromise from 1958.

01

Plain ASCII

No ü, no tone marks, no diacritics. v is the vowel /y/. Passports, URLs, SQL and old terminals will not eat it.

02

No contractions

iou, uei and uen are always written out. No iu/ui/un, and no “the main vowel disappears when you mark the tone”.

03

No rewrites

au is not written ao; ung is not written ong; iung is not written iong; ien is not written ian. Spelling does not hide history.

04

No silent glides

No y/w as orthographic initials. Zero-initial syllables are written with their vowel; an apostrophe separates syllables when needed: Xi'an → hi1'an1.

05

Apical vowel separate

The final in zi/ci/si/zhi/chi/shi/ri is written y. zhi and ji no longer share an i.

06

Tone after the syllable

1–4 for the four tones, 0 for neutral. Tone is part of the spelling, not an optional ornament.

Tone is a digit.

Not a mark above a vowel. Not a font. Not something an input method can swallow.

ToneDigitPitchExamplePinglish

Neutral0—吗 mama0
Why digits

Tone marks need a font, an input method and a typesetting system to cooperate. Digits do not. They sort, search, regex and fit in a URL, and they survived every terminal since 1985.

The cost: they are not beautiful. We accept the cost.

Syllable boundaries

Zero-initial syllables are written with their vowel. When confusion is possible, use an apostrophe:

Contrast02
Xi'an hi1'an1
xian hien1
Tian'anmen tien1'an1 men2
fang'an fang1'an4

Word spacing

Syllables inside a word may be joined; words are separated by spaces. Pinglish defines syllable boundaries, not a full orthography.

Sentence03
uo3 hi3 huan0 chy1 bei3 ging1 kau3 ia1.
I like eating Beijing roast duck.

05 / 07

What it costs.

There is no free redesign. Here is what this scheme explicitly gives up.

01

g/k/h change reading

Before i/v they are [tɕ/tɕʰ/ɕ]; elsewhere [k/kʰ/x]. One letter, two sounds. The payoff is deleting j/q/x entirely.

02

ien / ven spell the vowel

They follow the actual [ɛ] and give up the morphological parallel with an/uan. This is a “sound first” choice.

03

v as a vowel

In most languages v is a consonant; here it is /y/. The payoff is plain ASCII, one letter, no ü.

04

Tone digits

Less beautiful than tone marks. But typeable, searchable, sortable, reversible, and never missing from a font.

05

y for the apical vowel

It appears only after z/c/s/zh/ch/sh/r, so it cannot collide. For a beginner it is still a new rule.

06

What it does not solve

Polyphony, tone sandhi, regional varieties, name conventions, adoption cost. Spelling is a small part of the language problem.

The most important point: nobody is asked to adopt this. Pinyin is already in several generations of muscle memory, and any replacement costs far more than its theoretical gain. Pinglish is a reference point: it shows where the current scheme made choices, and who is still paying for them.

What it borrows.

No romanization starts from zero. Neither does this one.

1918Zhuyin fuhaoThree-part syllables and independent finals. Pinglish inherits the idea that a final should stand on its own.
1928Gwoyeu Romatzyhy for the apical vowel; tone shown in the spelling. Pinglish takes the first and gives up the second.
1931Latinized New Writing (Northern)zh/ch/sh paired with z/c/s; au and ung. Pinglish keeps both.
1958Hanyu PinyinInitials, most finals, the four-tone frame. The starting point.
2026PinglishPuts reversibility, typeability and romanization ahead of historical habit.
